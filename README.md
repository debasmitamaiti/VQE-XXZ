# VQE-XXZ- 
# Variational Quantum Eigensolver for the XXZ Heisenberg Chain

A study of two variational circuit architectures for the ground state of the
spin-1/2 XXZ Heisenberg chain, benchmarked against exact diagonalization.

**Central question:** a number-conserving RBS/Givens circuit plateaus above the true
ground-state energy. Why — and what fixes it?

**Answer, established numerically:** the RBS circuit can only build *Slater determinants*,
so it is capped at the Hartree–Fock energy. Adding an interaction (ZZ) gate and correcting
the reference-state symmetry reaches the exact ground state.

---

## Model

$$H = J\sum_{i} \left(S^x_i S^x_{i+1} + S^y_i S^y_{i+1} + \Delta\, S^z_i S^z_{i+1}\right)$$

with $S^\alpha = \sigma^\alpha/2$, open boundaries, $J = 1$, and $\Delta = 1$ (the isotropic
Heisenberg point) as the primary benchmark.

## Key results

| ansatz | L = 4 energy | error |
|---|---|---|
| exact diagonalization | −1.6160254038 | — |
| RBS / Givens | −1.5818190387 | 0.0342 |
| improved (XY + ZZ) | −1.6160254038 | ~1e−15 |

The RBS error is **not** an optimization failure. It is unchanged by extra circuit depth and
by a larger optimizer budget, and it coincides to ~1e−12 with an *independently computed*
best Slater determinant obtained by classical orbital optimization with no circuit involved.
It is a representation limit, and the residual is the correlation energy.

Two independent confirmations of the mechanism:

- **The RBS error vanishes at Δ = 0**, where the model maps to free fermions and its ground
  state genuinely *is* a Slater determinant — so there is no interaction to miss.
- **Under Jordan–Wigner**, the Givens and XY gates are generated by *bilinear* fermion
  operators (Gaussian-preserving), while the ZZ gate is generated by the *quartic* $n_i n_j$.
  Only ZZ leaves the Gaussian manifold.

### Finite-size scaling

The improved ansatz is more accurate at every size tested, with both given identical depth
and optimizer budget:

| L | RBS error | improved error |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.034206 | 0.000000 |
| 6 | 0.072676 | 0.000074 |
| 8 | 0.112885 | 0.001916 |
| 10 | 0.144639 | 0.034762 |
| 12 | 0.175767 | 0.080932 |
| 14 | 0.195490 | 0.106590 |

The advantage shrinks with L — the improved circuit has twice the parameters, so at a *fixed*
optimizer budget the extra optimization difficulty eventually outweighs the expressive gain.
Large-L values are budget-limited upper bounds, not converged results.

### Reference-state symmetry

The improved ansatz gates commute with chain reflection, so the reflection sector of the
reference state is conserved. The exact ground state's reflection eigenvalue alternates as
$(-1)^{L/2}$, and the plain Néel state sits in an equal mixture of both sectors — making the
target unreachable at any depth. The notebooks try both reference signs and keep the lower
energy, which is safe by the variational principle and needs no prior knowledge.

### Noise and error mitigation

Depolarizing gate noise and readout error, with three mitigation strategies. At depolarizing
strength p = 0.02:

| strategy | error | improvement |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 0.479 | 1.0× |
| readout mitigation | 0.329 | 1.5× |
| symmetry verification | 0.361 | 1.3× |
| zero-noise extrapolation | 0.031 | 15.5× |
| all three combined | 0.024 | 20.1× |

Symmetry verification is free: the ansatz cannot leave the $S^z = 0$ sector, so any
wrong-magnetization measurement is provably an error and is discarded.

## Method notes

- Exact benchmarks use sparse Lanczos diagonalization restricted to the conserved
  $S^z_{\rm tot} = 0$ sector, of dimension $\binom{L}{L/2}$ rather than $2^L$.
- Circuits are constructed and drawn with Qiskit; every gate matrix is verified against its
  analytic target to better than 1e-10 before use.
- The quantum side uses Qiskit (`QuantumCircuit`, `SparsePauliOp`, `StatevectorEstimator`);
  the classical optimization uses SciPy.
- The variational bound $E \ge E_0$ is asserted at every system size as a correctness check.

## Scope

This project applies established methods to a well-understood model. VQE, symmetry-preserving
ansätze, the Hamiltonian Variational Ansatz, and the error-mitigation techniques used here are
all published work — see the references in each notebook. It is intended as a rigorous,
reproducible study and teaching resource, not as a claim of new research results.
