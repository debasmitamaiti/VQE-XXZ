# VQE for the XXZ Heisenberg Chain

**Ansatz expressibility versus noise resilience — from exact simulation to IBM Quantum hardware.**

---

## The question

Two variational ansätze for the ground state of the spin-1/2 XXZ chain:

- **RBS / Givens** — number-conserving real rotations. Mathematically **capped** at the
  Hartree–Fock energy, −1.581819.
- **Improved (XY + ZZ)** — adds interaction gates and a reflection-symmetric reference state.
  Reaches the **exact** ground state, −1.616025.

In simulation the improved ansatz is strictly better. It also needs roughly **twice the
two-qubit gates**, and two-qubit gates are where hardware noise lives. So:

> **Does the improved ansatz's accuracy survive its gate cost on a real device — or does the
> cheaper, mathematically inferior circuit win in practice?**

**Answer: on `ibm_fez`, RBS wins at 8.5σ, and the reversal survives every mitigation strategy
tested.**

---

## Model

$$H = J\sum_{i} \left(S^x_i S^x_{i+1} + S^y_i S^y_{i+1} + \Delta\, S^z_i S^z_{i+1}\right)$$

with $S^\alpha = \sigma^\alpha/2$, open boundaries, $J = 1$, and $\Delta = 1$ (the isotropic
Heisenberg point) as the primary benchmark.

# Part I — Simulation: establishing the mechanism

## Why RBS plateaus

| ansatz | L = 4 energy | error |
|---|---|---|
| exact diagonalization | −1.6160254038 | — |
| RBS / Givens | −1.5818190387 | 0.0342 |
| improved (XY + ZZ) | −1.6160254038 | ~1e−15 |

The RBS error is **not** an optimization failure. It is unchanged by extra circuit depth and
by a larger optimizer budget, and it coincides to ~1e−12 with an *independently computed* best
Slater determinant obtained by classical orbital optimization with no circuit involved. It is
a representation limit, and the residual is the correlation energy.

Two independent confirmations:

- **The RBS error vanishes at Δ = 0**, where the model maps to free fermions and its ground
  state genuinely *is* a Slater determinant — there is no interaction to miss.
- **Under Jordan–Wigner**, the Givens and XY gates are generated by *bilinear* fermion
  operators (Gaussian-preserving), while the ZZ gate is generated by the *quartic* $n_i n_j$.
  Only ZZ leaves the Gaussian manifold.

## Reference-state symmetry

The improved ansatz gates commute with chain reflection, so the reflection sector of the
reference state is conserved. The exact ground state's reflection eigenvalue alternates as
$(-1)^{L/2}$, and the plain Néel state sits in an equal mixture of both sectors — making the
target **unreachable at any depth**. The notebooks try both reference signs and keep the lower
energy, which is safe by the variational principle and needs no prior knowledge.

## Finite-size scaling

Both ansätze given identical depth and optimizer budget:

| L | RBS error | improved error |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.034206 | 0.000000 |
| 6 | 0.072676 | 0.000074 |
| 8 | 0.112885 | 0.001916 |
| 10 | 0.144639 | 0.034762 |
| 12 | 0.175767 | 0.080932 |
| 14 | 0.195490 | 0.106590 |

The advantage shrinks with L: the improved circuit has twice the parameters, so at a *fixed*
optimizer budget the extra optimization difficulty eventually outweighs the expressive gain.
Large-L values are budget-limited upper bounds, not converged results.

## Simulated noise and mitigation

Density-matrix simulation with depolarizing gate noise and readout error. At depolarizing
strength p = 0.02:

| strategy | error | improvement |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 0.479 | 1.0× |
| readout mitigation | 0.329 | 1.5× |
| symmetry verification | 0.361 | 1.3× |
| zero-noise extrapolation | 0.031 | 15.5× |
| all three combined | 0.024 | 20.1× |

**Note for comparison with Part II:** this uses an idealized *depolarizing* noise model. On
real hardware the ranking of these methods changes substantially — see below. That difference
is itself one of the study's findings.

# Part II — Hardware: does the mechanism survive?

Circuits optimized noiselessly, **frozen**, and executed on **IBM `ibm_fez`** (Heron r2, 156
qubits). No optimization loop runs on hardware; each circuit is submitted once.

## 1. The ordering reversal

Ordering gap $\Delta = E(\text{improved}) - E(\text{RBS})$. Negative means the correct
ordering; the noiseless ideal is −0.0342.

| mitigation arm | gap | significance |
|---|---|---|
| raw (no mitigation) | **+0.1104 ± 0.0130** | 8.5σ |
| TREX (readout twirling) | **+0.1333 ± 0.0148** | 9.0σ |
| symmetry post-selection | **+0.0592 ± 0.0149** | 4.0σ |
| M3 + symmetry | **+0.0539 ± 0.0145** | 3.7σ |
| *noiseless ideal* | *−0.0342* | — |

Mitigation halves the gap but never reverses the sign. **TREX makes the gap worse**, because
readout mitigation helps shallow circuits disproportionately (62% error reduction for the
shallowest circuit against 28% for the deepest) — the cheaper circuit benefits more, widening
the very gap it was meant to close.

## 2. Mitigation ladder

Error measured against each circuit's *own* noiseless energy, so ansatz error and device error
are never conflated.

| circuit | 2q gates | ISA depth | raw | TREX | post-sel | M3 | **M3+sym** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| imp_d1 | 14 | 37 | 0.260 | 0.098 | 0.066 | 0.145 | **0.068** |
| rbs_d2 | 12 | 45 | 0.253 | 0.098 | 0.012 | 0.105 | **0.005** |
| imp_d2 | 25 | 75 | 0.398 | 0.266 | 0.105 | 0.280 | **0.093** |
| imp_d3 | 34 | 105 | 0.504 | 0.365 | 0.234 | 0.403 | **0.220** |

**The RBS circuit recovers to within 0.005 of its noiseless value** — a 53-fold error
reduction, essentially complete mitigation on a real device.

Free symmetry post-selection *outperforms* calibrated M3 readout mitigation (0.012 against
0.105 for rbs_d2): the ansatz cannot leave the $S^z_{\rm tot}=0$ sector, so any measured
bitstring with the wrong magnetization is provably an error. Observed leakage was 16–21%,
scaling with gate count.

**This inverts the simulated ranking of Part I**, where ZNE dominated and symmetry
verification was weakest. Under idealized depolarizing noise ZNE wins; under real device noise
the free symmetry method wins. Mitigation rankings do not transfer between noise models.

## 3. Controlled-depth experiment

The cleanest measurement in the study. Two circuits prepare the **identical target state**
(fidelity 1.000000000000, phase-aligned distance 2.8 × 10⁻⁷), differing only in compilation:

| | 2q gates | ISA depth | hardware error |
|---|---|---|---|
| imp_d2 | 25 | 75 | 0.398 |
| imp_d3 | 34 | 105 | 0.504 |

Because expressibility is held exactly fixed, the difference is pure implementation cost:

$$\epsilon(D_3)-\epsilon(D_2) = +0.1058 \pm 0.0136 \quad \text{for 9 extra two-qubit gates}$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad 0.0118 \pm 0.0015 \ \text{per two-qubit gate}$$

This is **5.4× the calibrated `cz` error** on the same qubit path (mean 0.00216), so gate
infidelity alone does not explain the penalty. The extra 30 layers of ISA depth also cost
decoherence: the deeper circuit runs 2.76 μs, roughly 5% of the shortest $T_2$ on the path
(52 μs on Q133).

## 4. What it would take for the improved ansatz to win

RBS is now mitigated **to within 0.005 of its mathematical ceiling** and cannot improve
further — the ceiling is a property of the ansatz, not of the hardware. That makes the
requirement precise:

> The improved ansatz must reduce its error below **0.039**. It currently achieves **0.093**.
> A further **2.4-fold error reduction** is required.

## 5. On zero-noise extrapolation

The ZNE arm was planned but **not executed**. It deserves explicit comment.

**It targets the error that remains.** TREX and M3 correct *readout* error. Symmetry
post-selection catches only errors that change the magnetization — $X$ and $Y$ errors are
detectable, but $Z$-type (phase) errors pass through untouched, since they leave the up-spin
count intact. What remains is precisely the coherent and phase error ZNE addresses.

**The required improvement is plausible but not comfortable.** ZNE typically delivers two- to
fivefold bias reduction; the threshold here is 2.4-fold. Genuinely uncertain.

**Two cautions.** ZNE reduces bias at the cost of *variance* — it extrapolates from a
difference of noisy quantities. With the gap at 3.7σ, it could shrink the gap while inflating
the uncertainty, leaving the comparison inconclusive. And ZNE handles *stochastic* noise far
better than *coherent* error: unitary folding $UU^\dagger U$ amplifies random errors
faithfully, but systematic over-rotation partially cancels under the inverse gate.

**Estimated cost:** ~189 s of quantum runtime, against ~61 s used by the entire study so far.

**Framing, either way.** If ZNE closes the gap: *the reversal is a mitigable artefact of noise
at this scale.* If it does not: *the reversal survives the full mitigation stack, so circuit
cost genuinely dominates expressibility on this hardware generation.* Both are meaningful
statements about the device.

## Method notes

- Exact benchmarks use sparse Lanczos diagonalization restricted to the conserved
  $S^z_{\rm tot} = 0$ sector, of dimension $\binom{L}{L/2}$ rather than $2^L$.
- Circuits are constructed and drawn with Qiskit; **every gate matrix is verified against its
  analytic target to better than 1e-10 before use.**
- The quantum side uses Qiskit (`QuantumCircuit`, `SparsePauliOp`, `StatevectorEstimator`);
  classical optimization uses SciPy directly, so the quantum–classical loop stays visible.
- The variational bound $E \ge E_0$ is asserted at every system size as a correctness check.

## Interpretation and limits

**What this establishes.** On current superconducting hardware, at this problem size, circuit
cost dominates expressibility. An ansatz that is *exact* in simulation loses to one that is
mathematically capped, because it needs roughly twice the two-qubit gates. Error mitigation
narrows the gap substantially but does not close it. And mitigation rankings measured under
simulated depolarizing noise do not carry over to real devices.

**What it does not establish.** One device, one problem size (L = 4), one anisotropy (Δ = 1),
a single session. This is a statement about *this hardware regime*, not about the ansätze in
general. On a sufficiently low-noise device the ordering should revert to the ideal one;
locating that crossover would need materially better two-qubit fidelity.

**Open items.** The ZNE arm has not been run. Symmetry post-selection is applied only to
$Z$-basis measurement settings, since magnetization is not directly readable in rotated
bases — extending it would require ancilla-based parity checks at additional circuit cost.

## Scope

This project applies established methods — VQE, symmetry-preserving ansätze, the Hamiltonian
Variational Ansatz, TREX and M3 readout mitigation, symmetry verification — to a
well-understood model. It is a reproducible study and teaching resource, not a claim of new
methodology.
